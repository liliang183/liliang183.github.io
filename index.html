Liang Li 李良

Following the dynamics of heavy-ion collisions, from first contact to the formation of new nuclei.

I study fusion, quasifission, and shell effects in reactions relevant to superheavy elements. My work uses time-dependent density functional theory and connects microscopic trajectories with reaction observables.

01 / Research

Microscopic dynamics,
observable outcomes.

A heavy-ion collision can lead to a compound nucleus or reseparate before equilibration. I study how nuclear structure, geometry, and dissipation shape that choice.

01

Fusion & superheavy nuclei

Connecting capture and post-capture dynamics to fusion probabilities and evaporation-residue production.

02

Quasifission & shell effects

Tracing how orientation, beam energy, and shell structure steer mass flow and fragment formation.

03

Microscopic methods

Using TDHF and nuclear energy-density functionals, with extensions for pairing, fluctuations, and particle-number projection.

03 / About

About me

I am a theoretical nuclear physicist at the School of Nuclear Science and Technology, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (UCAS), in Beijing. My research examines low-energy heavy-ion reactions with microscopic many-body methods.

I work with Lu Guo and have collaborated with Kyle Godbey and A. S. Umar on quasifission, tensor forces, and the dynamics of superheavy-element formation.
